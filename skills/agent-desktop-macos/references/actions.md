# macOS actions and keys

The full macOS rules for ref actions, menus and keys. The platform skill (`agent-desktop skills get platform`) has the short recipes.

**Headed actions.** `click`, `right-click`, `type`, `clear` and `scroll` use physical paths first, except a click on an item in the application's native menu bar hierarchy, which calls `AXPress` without moving the pointer. A headless click on these menu items also calls `AXPress`. Process-owned refs have no window to focus; other ref-target pointer actions focus the exact owning window and verify the hit target before delivery. A headless `right-click` can use `AXShowMenu` when the target provides it. `expand`, `collapse`, `set-value`, `select`, `toggle`, `check`, `uncheck`, `focus` and `scroll-to` stay semantic under `--headed`. `double-click`, `triple-click`, `hover` and `drag` need `--headed`. Raw `--xy` input never changes focus. A headless `click` on an item that publishes `AXOpen` and no `AXPress`, such as a file or folder item, opens it rather than only selecting it. When the item opens in place, the window takes the item's name as its title, which verifies the click, and the old view's refs are gone, so snapshot again. When no effect is seen, the click fails; snapshot before you click again, because the item may already be open. For a table or outline row (`row`, `treeitem`), a selection change can verify an otherwise unconfirmed `AXConfirm` or selection step, or a dispatched headed pointer click. The row must read as unselected before and selected after. The `selection_postcondition` step reports that read. Selection alone does not resolve an uncertain `AXOpen` or `AXPress`.

**Scroll into view.** A scroll area clips its content only on an axis where the content overflows it, judged by its `AXContentSize`, so an element of a scroll view that centres narrower content is not marked `offscreen` because of where the view reports its frame. A pointer action or `scroll-to` on a hidden target first runs the target's `AXScrollToVisible` when it advertises one. Otherwise each enclosing scroll area, innermost first, scrolls semantically, under `--headed` as well, on the axis that hides the target. An area that shows it cannot move further in a direction, because its scroll bar sits at that end or the target's bounds did not change, is not asked again. An area stops when its next step would reverse the last step that moved the target, so a step larger than the gap to the target is not followed by one that carries it back; a target that is only partly inside keeps scrolling the way it was going. The target counts as revealed only when a fresh read shows part of it visible through its scroll areas and its window, and that read decides the result: a reveal that ends visible succeeds even if a scroll step on the way failed, and one that ends hidden reports the last step's failure, or `kind: scroll_step_overshoot` when the last step that moved the target carried it past the viewport edge and the next would only carry it back. A blocked step on the other axis may come after that step, and the target may still be partly outside the viewport. A page scroll that a scroll area refuses with `kAXErrorCannotComplete` fails `ACTION_NOT_SUPPORTED` with `kind: scroll_action_unavailable`, `not_delivered`, only when all of these hold: the app answered well inside its messaging timeout; the area showed that it is at its end that way, because its scroll bar on that axis reads that end before and after the refusal or, with no bar on that axis, its `AXContentSize` fits inside it on every read after the refusal, since whether the content fits does not depend on the scroll position; and everything the scroll could move stayed readable and unchanged for 250 ms: the bar value, the frames of the area's first content children and of each one's first children, with the bar or at least two frames among them. Then you can safely scroll the other way or a different area. If any of them moved, the scroll counts as done. Otherwise the original error and its uncertain disposition stay: unchanged signals of an area that still has room are no proof, since a pinned header or a viewport-sized list stays put while its rows scroll, and an animated scroll can start late. A refusal that ran into its messaging timeout keeps `APP_UNRESPONSIVE` for the same reason, because the app may still scroll.

**In-place edits.** A row being renamed, as after Finder's New Folder, is edited by a separate text editor that floats over the row and holds focus. The editor has no window, so a window snapshot or `find --window-id` does not show it, and the row's own field may be hidden. Get it with `snapshot --app <App> --surface focused -i`: while the edit is open the surface root is the editor, a `textfield` holding the current name. `set-value` or `type` its ref (or the row's field, when the editor provably edits it). The result is `delivered_unverified` with `application_commit: "pending"` and `commit_key: "return"`. Commit with `press return --app <App>`, then check the result independently. Do not press each character with `press`; keys can be dropped.

**Select text, format it, save.** `select @<textfield> "<text>"` selects that exact text, case included, in a text field or text area. The text must occur once: when it occurs more than once, `select` fails with `AMBIGUOUS_TARGET` and `details.offsets`, and nothing changes, so add neighbouring words. When it is absent, the code is `ELEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Overlapping occurrences count, so `ana` in `banana` is ambiguous. When the field reports a different selection after the write, `select` fails `ACTION_FAILED` (`details.kind: selection_not_observed`); when the selection lands on the range but the field's text there changed after it was searched, it fails `ACTION_FAILED` (`details.kind: selection_text_changed`). Both are `retry: unsafe`; take a fresh snapshot and read the field's value and selection before deciding the next step. To format a few characters inside a word that repeats, select a run that occurs once, `press left --app <App>` to put the caret at its start, then `press right` and `press shift+right` as many times as needed. Then run the format command on the selection: find the menu item with `find --app <App> --surface menubar --name <Item> --exact` and `click` it, or press its shortcut. A format that has no menu item, such as strikethrough in a rich-text editor, is often in the Fonts panel (Format > Font > Show Fonts, `cmd+t`): list the panel with `list-windows --app <App>` and snapshot it by `--window-id`. There, choose `Single` with `select` on its strikethrough pop-up, or open the panel's Font Attributes menu button with `click` and click `Strikethrough` in `--surface menu`. Click that item headless: a headed `click` on a pop-up menu item outside the menu bar fails `ACTION_FAILED` before any input, because the item has no owning window. Save with `press cmd+s --app <App>`; the step `AXPress menu shortcut` with `data.details.menu_shortcut.item: "Save"` ran File > Save, which writes a document that already has a file in place, with no panel. Example for a document open in TextEdit:

```bash
agent-desktop snapshot --app TextEdit -i --compact --lines        # the body is a textfield ref
agent-desktop select @s8f3k2p9:e2 "The final paragraph, word for word."
agent-desktop press cmd+t --app TextEdit                           # shows the Fonts panel
agent-desktop list-windows --app TextEdit                          # the panel: title "Fonts", id w-NNNN
agent-desktop snapshot --window-id w-NNNN -i --compact --lines     # its strikethrough control
agent-desktop press cmd+s --app TextEdit
```

**Linked text fields.** `set-value` on a text field gives the field keyboard focus inside its window first, so the app's own edit handling runs: in an image-size sheet that keeps the aspect ratio, setting the height also updates the width. Read the linked field back before you confirm the sheet. The app is not brought forward.

**Judge delivery by observation.** AX return codes do not prove an effect. Finder returns success from an `AXConfirm` that does nothing. Snapshot again or use `get` and `is` to check the result.

**Menus.** `right-click` headless uses `AXShowMenu`. That call does not return while the menu it opened is tracking, so agent-desktop gives it about 250 ms, then reads the app's menu surface for up to 1.5 s: a menu that opened is a success with a verified step. `ACTION_FAILED` with `details.menu_observed: false` (and `details.perform_budget_ms`) means the app answered but no menu appeared yet; `APP_UNRESPONSIVE` means the app did not answer. Both carry `retry: unsafe`: do not retry, run `wait --menu --app App` or inspect the menu with `--surface menu`. Each fallback step sends nothing unless a menu read answers closed; a read that stays unreadable over a few tries counts as a menu being up. A pop-up menu window of the app on screen counts as a menu being up. In a window too large for the menu search (2,048 nodes), a step acts only when the window server lists no pop-up menu window of the app. A menu drawn inside an ordinary window and not exposed as an accessibility menu, such as a custom menu in a web-runtime app, is seen by neither read, so a second right-click can still reach it; this is unchanged from earlier releases. A fallback that selects the row before opening its menu has changed the selection, so if it then fails or no menu appears the result carries `retry: unsafe` (`delivered_unverified`, or `delivery_uncertain` when the menu action itself timed out). `select` opens its menu the same way. `menu` requires an open menu: if `snapshot --surface menu` returns `surface_absent` with `complete: true`, snapshot `menubar` when it exists, click its item without `--headed`, then snapshot `--surface menu`. An absent `menubar` can mean the app exposes no menu bar. A missing `complete`, `complete: false`, timeout or incomplete read is unknown, not absent. For combo boxes and menu buttons, use `select @ref "Option"`. To select a table or outline row, use `select @<row> "<label>"`, where the label is the row's name or the text of one of its cells, matched whole and ignoring case. It writes the container's `AXSelectedRows` to replace its selection, or falls back to the row's `AXSelected` on controls that do not accept that container write. Do not use it to add to a multi-selection. It verifies the selection by readback. An already-selected row writes nothing and returns `not_delivered`. A label the row does not carry fails `ELEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and a row that accepts neither write fails `ACTION_NOT_SUPPORTED`, both before any change. Selecting an item through a menu needs a closed-to-open transition, so dismiss any open menu first.

**Keys.** The primary modifier is `cmd`: `press cmd+c`, `press cmd+s`. Quit shortcuts such as `cmd+q`, `cmd+shift+q`, `cmd+alt+esc`, `ctrl+cmd+q` and `cmd+shift+delete` return `POLICY_DENIED` unless you pass `--force`. To quit an app, use `close-app`. Punctuation keys work in combos, as the symbol (`,` `.` `/` `;` `'` `[` `]` `\` `-` `=` `` ` ``) or by name (`comma`, `period`, `slash`, `semicolon`, `quote`, `leftbracket`, `rightbracket`, `backslash`, `minus`, `equal`, `grave`). Each one is sent with the key that types it in the active keyboard layout, so `press cmd+,` opens Settings on ANSI and JIS keyboards alike. When the app's focus is in a panel that another process serves, such as a Save sheet, `press --app` sends the keys to that process and skips the app's menu shortcuts; see `references/troubleshooting.md`, Save into a given folder.

**Menu shortcuts.** `press` with `cmd` in the chord and `--app` or `--window-id` first reads the app's menu bar for items whose `AXMenuItemCmdChar`, `AXMenuItemCmdModifiers` and, for named keys such as `delete`, `AXMenuItemCmdVirtualKey` equal the chord. Exactly one enabled match is pressed with `AXPress`, headless included: the step is `AXPress menu shortcut` (`semantic_api`) and `data.details.menu_shortcut` names its `menu` and `item`. AppKit enables window commands such as New Folder or Close Window only while the app is frontmost, so in a background app they read as disabled and do not run. When the only match is disabled and the app is in the background, `press` fails with `ACTION_FAILED`, `details.kind: menu_item_disabled_in_background`, `not_delivered` and safe to retry: run `focus-window` and `press` again, or, only if the user allows physical input, rerun it with `--headed` instead. In every other miss the chord is sent as keys, and `data.details.menu_shortcut.skipped` says why when the lookup applied: `ambiguous` (two enabled items share the chord), `disabled` (frontmost app, only disabled matches), `window_overlay` (the focused window shows a sheet, dialog, alert or popover, which gets the chord before the menu bar does), `menu_open`, `lookup_incomplete` (with `stage`, `kind` and the `menu` where it stopped) or `not_pressable`. The walk has 1 s and 2,048 elements; each submenu read gets 100 ms, and a submenu that misses it is listed in `unread_menus` and skipped. `visited`, `unread_after_budget` and `slowest_read` describe the walk. A read is named `Menu > Item`, with the submenu that holds it in between when there is one, or by its position (`File > item 7`) when the read that returns its title is the one that failed. A command chord on a character key (`cmd+s`, `cmd+b`) runs only through a menu item: posted to an app that is not frontmost, it does nothing. So when no enabled menu item takes such a chord and the app is in the background, headless `press` fails with `ACTION_FAILED`, `details.kind: command_chord_unrouted_in_background`, `not_delivered` and safe to retry: `click` the menu item, or, only if the user allows physical input, rerun it with `--headed` instead. Do not do both, or the command runs twice. Navigation chords such as `cmd+right`, `cmd+up` and `alt+shift+down` still reach the focused text view of a background app. After a `skipped` result in a frontmost app, check the effect, and fall back to `click` on the menu item. Pressing a menu item can bring the app forward, just as a headless `click` on one can. An unscoped `press` always sends keys.

**Return and Escape on a sheet or dialog.** `press return`, `enter` or `escape` sends a real key first and checks that the sheet's or dialog's own window left the screen. A sheet that only resized has not reacted. A success is `delivered_verified` with `details.modal_key.effect: "observed"`; `effect: "unobservable"` with `delivered_unverified` means the sheet's window could not be told apart, so check the result yourself. When nothing changed it fails with `ACTION_FAILED`, `retry: unsafe` and `effect: "none_observed"`, even though the key was sent. Do not repeat the same press: snapshot the surface with `--surface sheet -i` (a dialog: `--surface alert -i`) and click its default button by ref when `default_button` is `true`. Without one, after `return` or `enter` click the button that confirms it, such as OK, Go or Save, by ref, never Close or Cancel, which discard the edit; after `escape` click its Close or Cancel button. Instead of that click, and only if the user allows physical input, send the key once with `--headed` so the window takes focus. While a panel that another process serves holds the focus, such as a Save panel (`details.keyboard_target.route: "hosted_view"`), this check does not run: the key goes to that process and `keyboard_target.effect` reports the result, with no `modal_key`.

**Clicks inside a sheet or alert.** A `click` on an element inside a sheet, or inside a window whose subrole is a dialog or alert, is judged by that surface. When the press alone could not be verified, agent-desktop watches the surface's own window for up to 750 ms and counts it closed only when it is off screen on two consecutive reads. That window belongs to the one sheet that held the button, so a second sheet with the same title stacked behind it never answers for it. `delivered_verified` with `details.surface_after_press: {"surface": "sheet", "effect": "closed"}` means the sheet closed. `delivered_unverified` with `effect: "still_showing"` means the press was delivered and the sheet is still open: either the button does not close it or the app ignored the press. Snapshot it again with `--surface sheet -i` (a dialog: `--surface alert -i`) and decide from what it shows; do not click the same button again on a guess. `effect: "unobservable"` means the sheet has no window of its own to watch, so check `list-surfaces` yourself. `data.surfaces` still lists every sheet left open, including others stacked on the same window.

**Notification Center.** The notification commands drive Notification Center through accessibility, with the rules in the core `commands-system` reference. When agent-desktop opens the center, it closes it afterwards and restores the previous app focus; if that cleanup fails after a dismiss or action was sent, the mutation's result stands (`notification-action` adds `details.list_restore: "unverified"`; `dismiss-notification` and `dismiss-all-notifications` add `data.list_restore: "unverified"`) and you close the center with Escape. A dismiss that fails after any dismiss strategy was sent, for example when the re-read loses accessibility permission, reports `retry: unsafe`; list again before deciding. Pass the entry's `--key` from `list-notifications` on a single dismiss or action, because the stack shifts. Use `wait --notification --app "App"` for banners that vanish before you list them.

**Cursor overlay.** `cursor-overlay enable` draws the agent cursor in a click-through window. It never moves the OS pointer or activates an app. Only the CLI starts the renderer, under any file name; a process that loads the library, such as an FFI host, never starts one and draws no cursor. The macOS motion and image limits are in the core `commands-system` reference.
